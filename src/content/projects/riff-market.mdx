## 🧠 Problem Statement

Buying and selling music equipment involves more than displaying a catalog.
Customers need useful search and filters, sellers need inventory and order
workflows, and administrators need a dependable way to moderate listings.
Purchases can also contain products from multiple sellers, making stock,
payment, fulfillment, and notification state more complex than a conventional
single-store checkout.

**RiffMarket** brings these workflows into one responsive marketplace for
guitars, keyboards, pedals, and accessories. Customers can discover gear, manage
a cart, place purchases, save favorites, and write reviews. Sellers can publish
listings and process their portion of an order, while administrators control
listing approval and platform access.

RiffMarket is a full-stack TypeScript application built with **TanStack Start**.
File-based TanStack Router routes and server functions live in the same
codebase, while TanStack Query manages server state and Zustand handles focused
client state such as the cart, session-aware UI, notifications, theme, and
dialogs.

The codebase has evolved beyond a feature-only structure into domain modules for
`accounts`, `listings`, `ordering`, `reviews`, `notifications`, and `media`.
Business rules are kept in domain and application layers, while Prisma-backed
adapters implement persistence. Shared value objects model concepts such as
money, currency, image assets, actors, and result states without coupling core
rules directly to the UI or database.

### 🛒 Multi-Seller Ordering

A customer checkout creates one purchase and separates its items into seller
orders. Immutable snapshots preserve listing, seller, price, currency, and
shipping data even if a listing or account later changes. Each seller can then
move their own order through processing, shipping, delivery, or cancellation
without changing another seller's fulfillment state.

### 🛡️ Marketplace Trust and Media Lifecycle

Listings follow a moderation lifecycle of pending, approved, declined, or
withdrawn. Cloudinary stores listing and profile images, while a durable media
cleanup job queue tracks retries and prevents abandoned assets from silently
accumulating after listings or accounts are removed.

### 🧪 Testing Strategy

The repository includes unit and component tests with Vitest, database-backed
Prisma integration tests, browser tests, and Playwright end-to-end coverage.
Dedicated test database safeguards prevent integration tests from running
against a non-test database by mistake.

The schema separates a customer's overall purchase from the individual seller
orders required for fulfillment. Snapshot fields retain the commercial record
even when listings or accounts later change, while reviews, favorites,
notifications, and cleanup jobs remain independently queryable.

## 🚀 Key Features

- Customer, seller, and administrator authentication and authorization
- Search, category, brand, condition, and price-range filtering
- Listing creation, image upload, inventory, and approval workflows
- Shopping cart and transactional multi-seller checkout
- Seller-specific fulfillment and order status management
- Favorites, product reviews, and user notifications
- Profile settings, theme preferences, and safe account deletion
- Cloudinary image lifecycle management with a retryable cleanup queue
- Unit, integration, browser, and end-to-end test suites

## 🧩 Challenges & Learnings

The most important design challenge was preserving correctness across a
multi-seller purchase. Stock changes, purchase records, seller orders, item
snapshots, and notifications must succeed as one coherent operation. Modeling
these rules outside route components made the workflow easier to test and
reduced the risk of UI or persistence details leaking into core marketplace
logic.
